# Five-slide executive narrative

## Slide 1 — A small recovery routine can test a high-volume employee-experience risk

- Public feedback indicates recurring workload, scheduling and recovery concerns.
- The evidence is directional, not representative.
- Decision: approve a four-week Recovery Checkpoint Pilot on one shift.

## Slide 2 — Workload and recovery is the largest theme and has the highest negative count

- 78 theme-tagged records, including 47 positive, 25 neutral and six negative.
- Compensation and benefits is more consistently positive.
- The mixed workload signal suggests favorable conditions can coexist with operational strain.

## Slide 3 — Inconsistent shift-level routines are a testable contributing factor

- Demand peaks, break coverage and task rotation are managed locally.
- Problems may surface too late when teams lack a predictable escalation moment.
- A routine checkpoint is more actionable than a broad policy change at this evidence level.

## Slide 4 — Two brief checkpoints create early visibility without redesigning the shift

- Start of shift: flag expected pressure and confirm recovery coverage.
- Mid-shift: identify missed breaks, fatigue concerns and rotation needs.
- Guardrails: no individual performance use, aggregated reporting and defined escalation paths.

## Slide 5 — Test for four weeks, then stop, revise or scale

- Owner: site operations; partners: HR, safety, workforce planning and people analytics.
- Measures: execution fidelity, break reliability, pulse score, absence, productivity and safety.
- Decision gate: scale only if employee-experience measures improve without breaching guardrails.
