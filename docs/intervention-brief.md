# Recovery Checkpoint Pilot

## Decision requested

Approve a four-week pilot on one fulfillment-center shift or operating area, with a named operations owner and weekly review cadence.

## Problem hypothesis

Public feedback suggests that workload pressure, scheduling friction and insufficient recovery may create avoidable strain for some frontline employees. The evidence does not establish company-wide prevalence or causality, but it is strong enough to justify a small test.

## Intervention

Introduce two brief, manager-led recovery checkpoints:

1. **Start-of-shift readiness check:** confirm staffing constraints, break coverage and foreseeable workload peaks.
2. **Mid-shift recovery check:** identify missed or delayed breaks, clarify task rotation and escalate emerging safety or fatigue concerns.

The checkpoint is a lightweight operating routine, not a medical assessment or employee-surveillance mechanism. Individual responses should not be used for performance management.

## Target population

Begin with one full-time frontline cohort on a shift where managers can reliably run both checkpoints. Participation in pulse feedback should be voluntary and aggregated.

## Ownership

- **Accountable owner:** site operations leader
- **Execution owner:** shift manager
- **Partners:** HR, safety and workforce planning
- **Measurement support:** people analytics

## Success measures

| Measure | Direction | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Checkpoint completion | ≥ 90% | Confirm implementation fidelity |
| Missed or delayed breaks | Decrease | Measure recovery reliability |
| Workload-manageability pulse | Improve | Capture employee experience |
| Unplanned absence | No deterioration | Monitor workforce impact |
| Productivity | Within agreed guardrail | Protect operational performance |
| Safety incidents | No deterioration | Protect employee safety |

Baseline definitions and thresholds should be agreed before launch. Results should be reported only at cohort level.

## Pilot sequence

- **Week 0:** establish baselines, train managers and confirm escalation paths.
- **Weeks 1–2:** run checkpoints and review implementation issues twice weekly.
- **Weeks 3–4:** stabilize the routine and collect outcome measures.
- **End of Week 4:** decide whether to stop, revise, extend or scale.

## Risks and safeguards

- **Check-the-box execution:** use a simple checklist and completion audit.
- **Manager burden:** keep each checkpoint under five minutes.
- **Employee distrust:** explain purpose, voluntary feedback and data boundaries.
- **Operational disruption:** start with one shift and protect productivity guardrails.
- **Misinterpretation:** treat the result as pilot evidence, not proof of causality.
